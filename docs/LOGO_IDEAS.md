## The bar

Not taste. Five things, four of them from §4 and §3 and one of them learned the hard way in §4a.

### What the renderer said — 2026-08-23

Every drawing on this page has been rasterised at 16, 32 and 180 px on a white ground and **looked at**, which the bar says to do and which nobody had done
before this date. It changed the decision. The findings below outlive the marks they were found on, which is why they stay after two of those marks were
dropped.

**The overflowing calendar cell fails criterion 1.** At 16 px it is a clipboard, not a calendar: the two hanger ticks merge into the top edge and become a
clamp, the three entry lines close up into a grey block, and the escaping line — the entire idea — is a fuzzy stub. At 180 px it still reads as a notepad
rather than a date square. It was never an argument against the _idea_ — that was the strongest conceptual case anyone made here — only against the drawing,
which never delivered it. **This is the finding that changed the shape of the whole decision**, since §4b had recommended the cell on reasoning no rendering
supported. It is kept after the idea was dropped because the lesson is the rule at the bottom of this page: draw it, render it at 16 px, look at it.

**The beanie needed six attempts, and four of them read as a bell, a cloche or a table lamp.** The mark is dropped, but one transferable thing came out of
it: an object is often legible only because of a line that implies what is _not_ drawn. A beanie with no head in it works only when the fold line sags, because
the sag is where the head would be — straighten it and the same silhouette becomes a plinth. Worth remembering the next time a mark leaves something out.

**The negative-space EJ survives 16 px, and the construction is why.** A hole in a solid shape stays legible far longer than a drawn letterform, because the
eye reads the surrounding mass rather than the stroke. Three versions: a circular badge wastes the corners and forces the letters smaller, and the first square
filled edge to edge and went soft — the adopted version is pulled in and thinned. The J's hook has to be a real quarter-circle; a short tail reads as a
lowercase `l`.

**The badge takes the stamp's ink, and needs no separate icon variant.** Drawing the badge distressed was proposed to stop the badge and the stamp being two
visual languages, and the renders said it works — but only with the right technique, and the first one was wrong in an instructive way. **Punching round holes
through the mask reads as foam at 512 px**: uniform circles look like bubbles, not ink. What works is the inverse — irregular rotated **ellipses filled with
mid-greys**. A mask is luminance-based, so grey erodes _partially_ and the ink thins rather than being punched out, which is what ink actually does.

Two constraints turned out to be load-bearing: **flecks must stay off the letters** (distress across a counter closes it, and a closed counter is an unreadable
letter), and **the silhouette must never be eroded** — bite the outline and the rounded square stops reading as an app icon. Because the silhouette survives,
**at 16 px the inked badge is indistinguishable from the clean one**; the texture only appears above ~180 px. The ink is therefore free at every size.

**The stamp's frame and typeface are not free, and testing them separately is what showed it.** Putting the club stamp's Helvetica Bold and double frame on the
badge produces a convincing sibling of the stamp at 180 px and a muddy one at 16 px, for two independent reasons: a 2.4-unit frame is 1.2 px there and renders
as a grey ring rather than a line, and Helvetica Bold's E has thinner arms than the hand-drawn 3-unit E, so the mark reads _"LJ"_. **A frame and two legible
letters do not both fit in 16 px** — one SVG unit is half a pixel, which is arithmetic rather than craft. So the framed badge is the badge **everywhere except the
favicon**, which keeps the geometric frameless construction; 24 px, the header's real size, was measured and is comfortably clear. The geometric letters carry a second advantage that only applies to the icon —
they depend on no font, and `favicon.svg` is served to browsers _as SVG_.

**The club stamp is a lockup and the render proves it rather than asserting it.** At 560 px it is a door stamp. At 96 px wide "EVENT JUNKIE" still reads and
**"BERLIN" is already a smudge**; at 16 px the whole thing is a grey rectangle. That is not a flaw to fix — distress is fine detail by definition — it is the
reason this pairs with a separate glyph for the icon slots instead of being asked to fill them.

### The stamp typeface, and the corner question

**A slab serif is what "stamp font" means here.** Six faces were set as the badge and rendered side by side rather than picked by reputation: **Rockwell Bold**
wins on even, heavy slab serifs — the office-stamp letter. **American Typewriter** is a close second with a more characterful J and is a legitimate swap.
**Copperplate** is too spindly, since its spurs are fine detail and fine detail is what small sizes eat. **Impact** is too cramped, with E and J nearly
colliding, and reads shipping crate rather than stamp. **DIN Condensed** is clean and pleasingly German but reads as _signage_, not as ink on paper. **Courier
New** is the passport-stamp cliché and its serifs are too thin to hold up.

**The trade-off to decide, not to overlook.** The club stamp uses Helvetica Bold, so the Helvetica badge matches the lockup exactly. A slab reads _more_ like a
rubber stamp on its own and _less_ like the stamp already drawn. Choosing slab therefore implies moving the club stamp to the same slab, or accepting that the
badge and the lockup are cousins rather than siblings.

**Both slab variants depend on a font**, which the geometric construction exists to avoid. Outline the text before shipping — the same fix the club stamp
carries — and note that a slab variant cannot be `favicon.svg` until it is outlined, since browsers are served the SVG itself.

### The split badge, and the licence question underneath the typeface

**The split inversion — E dark on light, J light on dark, divided down the middle — is the strongest badge construction drawn so far**, and the reason is not
decoration. A real stamp is a single block of ink, so a mark that is half ink and half paper looks like something _pressed_ rather than drawn. It also fixes the
inertness every EJ lockup has: two letters side by side do nothing, and here they are doing opposite things. Drawn, and **retired 2026-08-23** when the stamp badge took the display slot — recoverable from git as
`docs/branding/mark-ej-badge-split.svg`.

**It is display-only.** At 24 px it still reads; at 16 px the split becomes a smudge and both letters lose their grounds. The favicon keeps the plain frameless
badge, and this is comfortably the busiest construction on the page.

**The typeface in it is a placeholder, and replacing it is a licensing decision before it is an aesthetic one.** Two punk / rubber-stamp faces were proposed:

| Face             | Designer     | Licence                                                    |
| ---------------- | ------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Dharma Punk**  | junkohanhero | **Free for personal use only.** Commercial: €15 / $20      |
| **RUBBER STAMP** | Billy Argel  | **Free for personal use only.** Commercial: billyargel.com |

**Neither may be used in this logo as downloaded**, because Event Junkie is a public product rather than a personal one. **Outlining the glyphs does not change
that** — outlining is a technical step, not a grant of rights, and it is worth stating plainly because _"we converted it to paths"_ is the usual reason people
believe they are in the clear. Two honest routes:

- **Buy the commercial licence.** €15 for Dharma Punk is trivial against what this asset has to survive, and it is the right answer if that face is the one.
- **Use an SIL OFL face**, which permits commercial and logo use outright with no purchase and no record to keep. Closest to a rubber stamp: **Rubik
  Distressed**. A distressed typewriter: **Special Elite**. A stencil serif: **Stardos Stencil**. All three are on Google Fonts, which this project already
  self-hosts from via `@fontsource`.

**This is the same class of check as §4a and deserves the same seriousness.** A mark cleared against every trademark register and then set in a font nobody
licensed has been cleared of the wrong risk.

### Rubik Distressed, and two traps it exposed — decided 2026-08-23

**The face is [Rubik Distressed](https://fonts.google.com/specimen/Rubik+Distressed), SIL OFL 1.1.** Commercial and logo use permitted outright; nothing to buy,
nothing to record. It replaces the Rockwell placeholder and supersedes the slab-versus-Helvetica question entirely.

**Trap one: a renderer substitutes silently.** With the font downloaded but not installed, `rsvg-convert` rendered the `<text>` badge in clean Helvetica — and
it _looked fine_. Nothing warned, nothing errored. It was only caught by rendering the same string with a deliberately nonexistent family name and finding the
two files byte-identical. **A logo can be wrong in a way nothing complains about.** The fix is the one already required for delivery: the glyphs are extracted
with `fontTools` and embedded as `<path>`, so the file is self-contained and cannot fall back.

**Trap two: a distressed face has a high floor, and it is much higher than it looks.** Rendered as a true-size ladder:

| Size   | Verdict                                                    |
| ------ | ---------------------------------------------------------- |
| 32 px  | an illegible blob                                          |
| 48 px  | the J is guessable, the E is mush                          |
| 64 px  | both letters resolve, still rough                          |
| 96 px  | **comfortable** — distress reads as distress, not as noise |
| 128 px | good                                                       |

So the identity has **three size rungs**, not two: drawn letters at 16 px and 24 px, set letters from 96 px. That is normal practice for a distressed identity
rather than a compromise — the drawn letters are what the distressed ones look like once the distress stops being resolvable.

**No wear is applied over the letters any more.** The face supplies it, and layering the mask erosion on top turned them to mud. Only the frame is worn, which
also leaves the eye one clean edge to read the silhouette against.

**Four files were retired** as a consequence, all recoverable from git: the two Rockwell slab variants and the two Helvetica framed ones existed to test the
frame and the typeface, and both questions are now answered.

### The lockup moves to Rubik Distressed too — and a whole word is more forgiving than two letters

**Tested and adopted 2026-08-23.** The club stamp's wordmark is now the same face as the display badge, so lockup and badge are one object rather than cousins,
which was the whole reason for moving it off Helvetica.

**The counter-intuitive finding: the lockup's floor is far lower than the badge's.** The two-letter badge needs 96 px; the twelve-letter lockup is readable at
**240 px wide** and comfortable from 300 px, despite carrying the same distress. **A whole word carries its own shape**, so degraded glyphs still resolve
against it — where two isolated initials have nothing to lean on. More letters is more legible, not less, which is the opposite of the intuition that a longer
string is harder to set small.

**"BERLIN" stays clean, and that is the real finding from testing it.** Set in Rubik Distressed the secondary line degrades into a dotted row of specks well
before the wordmark does — at 460 px wide the wordmark still reads and BERLIN is already gone. A clean secondary line is also how real stamps are built, and the
contrast is what makes the distress look deliberate rather than like a printing fault.

**The wordmark is outlined; BERLIN is not yet.** That takes the lockup from two blockers to one: the secondary line should be set in **Geist** — the site's own
face, already self-hosted — and then outlined. Until then that single line renders in whatever the machine has.

### The badge as the lockup minus the words — adopted for display, 2026-08-23

Same −2.5° tilt, same face, same square corners, same frame-only wear, and **one frame instead of two**: a double rule reads cleanly across 320 units and closes
into a thick band across 32. Drawn as [`mark-ej-badge-stamp.svg`](branding/mark-ej-badge-stamp.svg).

**Sizing was matched by density, not arithmetic**, and that turned out to be the whole job. Three settings were rendered directly against the lockup: at 13/1.2
tracking the letters float in the box; at 14/0.8 they are comfortable but looser than the wordmark; at **15.5/0.6** they fill the frame the way _EVENT JUNKIE_
fills its own. Only the last reads as the same family rather than as two stamps that happen to share a face. Copying a frame weight and a tilt is not enough —
**the letters have to occupy their frame the same way.**

**This displaces the split badge from the display slot.** The split is the stronger idea taken alone — the half-ink, half-paper inversion is what a pressed
stamp actually looks like — but it is the worse match for a lockup that is a plain framed wordmark. Both are drawn; only one is assigned.

### The small badge, and why the icon rung cannot just be a downscale

**Three files retired 2026-08-23**: the split badge, and the two geometric badges that predate the typeface decision. All recoverable from git.

That left the 16 px and 24 px slots empty, which is not a tidy-up — **the display badge cannot fill them.** Rubik Distressed is illegible at 32 px and a blob
below it, so the favicon and the header cannot be a downscale of the stamp badge; they need simplified letterforms. That is ordinary practice for a distressed
identity rather than a compromise: the drawn letters are what the set ones look like once the distress stops being resolvable.

[`mark-ej-badge-small.svg`](branding/mark-ej-badge-small.svg) is that file. It **keeps** the −2.5° tilt and light edge wear, so it still
belongs to the stamp family, and **drops the frame** — measured, a framed tilted drawn-letter badge is mush at 16 px, because a 2.4-unit rule is 1.2 px there
and its inset steals the room the letters need.

**It reads clearly at 24 px and is marginal at 16 px**, resolving as a dark square with an EJ implied rather than stated. That is the normal condition of a
16 px favicon and it beat every alternative tested, but it is worth knowing rather than discovering. If it proves too soft in a real tab, **the first two things
to drop are the tilt and the wear** — both are sub-pixel noise at that size and neither carries meaning there.

**The single-ink reproduction variant went with the retirements.** Nothing is lost: this badge is already one flat colour, so it _is_ the single-ink case.

### The tagline in the stamp — drawn, and English-only

[`…-club-stamp-tagline-en.svg`](branding/lockup-club-stamp-tagline-en.svg) swaps _BERLIN_ for the tagline. It works: laddered at 460, 380, 300
and 240 px it is readable at 240 and comfortable from 300 — **the same floor as the BERLIN variant**, because twenty-six characters do not cost what they look
like they should when the caption is a clean face rather than a distressed one.

Sizing was picked against two alternatives. At 12 with 1.6 tracking the caption crowds the inner frame edge to edge; two lines at 11.5 collide with each other
and with the frame. At **10 with 3.4 tracking** it reads as a caption rather than as a second headline, which is the job a stamp's lower line does.

**Adopted for the hero on 2026-08-23, which forced a second file.** The live tagline beneath the hero is removed, so the stamp is now the only
place it appears — which makes the caption localised artwork. `/de` gets its own file rather than the English one, because §8 is explicit that German is the
brand speaking rather than a translation layer.

**And the German file is not the English one with the words swapped.** _VON BERLIN KRIEGST DU NIE GENUG_ is 31 characters against the English 26; at the English
settings it overflows the inner frame, clipping _VON_ and pushing _GENUG_ into the right rule. Rendered at three settings, 9.5 with 2.0 tracking fits with
margin and still reads as a caption rather than fine print. Each locale has its own caption sizing and neither number transfers.

The _BERLIN_ variant survives as the language-neutral option for any surface that needs one.

### Rounded corners were an app-icon idiom, and iOS was the argument against them — squared 2026-08-23

**Decided: every badge and the lockup are square-cornered.** Rendered square against rounded, the square-cornered badge reads as a **rubber stamp** and the
rounded one reads as an **app icon**. Real stamps and official
seals are hard-cornered; the rounded rectangle is borrowed from a different object, and it quietly works against the concept.

**The platform reason people round them is backwards.** iOS applies its own **squircle** mask to `apple-touch-icon` — a superellipse with continuous curvature,
not a rounded rectangle — so a full-bleed **square** image is what it wants. Pre-rounding produces _double-rounded_ corners with a visible gap at the edges. A
plain `rx` rounded rect never matches the squircle anyway. So iOS is a reason **not** to round, not a reason to.

Where it genuinely is a free choice: `favicon.svg` and `.ico` in a browser tab are not masked, and neither is the badge sitting in the header. _(macOS is the
exception that proves it — macOS app icons are **not** auto-masked and do need their rounding baked in.)_

## Contributed 2026-08-23 — a second pass

Eight directions were proposed in one batch. Four were already on this page in some form (ticket stub, EJ monogram, the Trainspotting poster language, and a
character head), and the batch's own closing note — _don't make it a music app; the differentiator is obsessive discovery of events_ — **independently reaches
§4b's conclusion**, which picked the overflow cell for being the only candidate about events rather than about music. Two passes converging on that is the
strongest signal on this page.

Two corrections to the framing it arrived with, before the ideas themselves:

- **"Music hunter" is narrower than the product.** The scope rule is _if a Berlin venue puts it on a stage in the evening, it is in scope_ —
  [EVENT_SCOPE.md](EVENT_SCOPE.md) — which includes the comedy and the quiz night. A mark that commits to music narrows what the importers actually cover.
- **"Hunter" sells the opposite of the proposition.** The value of an aggregator is that you _stop_ hunting; somebody already did it, on a schedule, across
  every source. §2 is explicit that the app is the source, not the search box.

### The club stamp

The door stamp on the back of your hand, as the wordmark. **The best of the batch**, and the only one that is Berlin without reaching for a Fernsehturm.
Naturally one ink, says _you got in_ rather than _here is an app_, and carries the §3 voice without a word of copy.

**It is a lockup, not a mark, and that is the whole point of it** — see the render note above. It pairs with a glyph for the icon slots rather than filling
them. Drawn as [`lockup-club-stamp.svg`](branding/lockup-club-stamp.svg), with two things flagged in the file that must happen before it could
ship: **the text has to be converted to outlines** (it is `<text>` in a system font stack today, which for a logo is a defect rather than a risk), and **the
distress has to be redrawn by hand** — the algorithmic specks read as uniform damage, where real stamp distress clusters at the edges and thins rather than
punches.

The 2.5-degree tilt should survive any redraw. A stamp that is level reads as a logo pretending to be a stamp.

### The negative-space EJ

The monogram already parked as §4 direction #2, drawn in the one construction that solves the app icon: the letters as holes in a badge rather than as strokes
on a ground. Drawn, and **retired 2026-08-23** once the typeface decision superseded it — recoverable from git as
`docs/branding/mark-ej-badge.svg`.

**Its strength is mechanical, not aesthetic.** It is the only candidate here that needs no padding — the badge already _is_ the rounded square iOS will mask —
and negative space is the construction that degrades most gracefully at small sizes. **Its weakness is unchanged from §4:** a monogram says nothing. It is the
right answer if the mark's job is to be an icon and the wordmark does the talking, and the wrong one if the mark is supposed to mean something.

One implementation note that will bite: the mask needs a unique id per instance. `PulseMark.vue` already learned this with its gradient and uses `useId()`; two
badges on one page sharing a mask id renders the second as a solid block.

### J as a magnifying glass

**Not recommended.** It fails two criteria and one of them is strategic rather than visual.

A magnifier is among the most-used shapes in software, so criterion 4 is gone before the drawing starts. But the real objection is criterion 5: it advertises
searching to people who came here to stop searching. The product's claim is coverage — 86 sources, on a schedule — and a magnifying glass draws the work the
product removes.

### Event signal — sources converging into one feed

**Conceptually the most accurate idea on this page, and the weakest execution.** It draws exactly what the importer does.

That is the problem. Hub-and-spoke is _the_ integration-product cliché — every ETL, iPaaS and API vendor has one — so criterion 4 fails hard. And it is an
engineer's view of the product: users do not care that listings converge, they care what is on tonight. Six nodes and their connecting lines are also far too
many elements to survive 16 px.

**Worth keeping for something else:** it would make a good explanatory diagram on the About page, where the mechanism is genuinely interesting and has room.

### The mascot, which is not the mark

A small obsessive creature with a stack of tickets. **As a logo it fails 16 px** on the same detail argument as the character head, and headphones would walk
straight into the "too music app" trap the batch itself warns about.

**As a mascot that is not the logo it is one of the better ideas here**, and the distinction is worth writing down because it keeps getting collapsed. §3
already asks for the voice to show up in the empty state, the 404 and the loading copy — that is a place for illustration, at a size where detail survives, and
_"Your Junkie found 37 new events"_ is exactly the register §3 describes. A creature also sidesteps what killed the beanie head: it has no age, no gender and no
subculture, so it commits the brand to no demographic at all.

**Kept out of the mark decision entirely.** It is a #374 question at the earliest, and probably a post-launch one.

## The other live ideas

Held here in one line each. The decision itself is [BRANDING.md](BRANDING.md) §4b.

- **Type-only** — no mark, "Event Junkie" set with one deliberate typographic move. Cheapest, ages best, cannot collide with anybody's icon, and a single
  letter becomes the app icon. **Never drawn**, and the decision went past it — §4b's runner-up was judged against drawn work from memory, which is not a
  comparison. If the stamp system is ever reopened, draw this one first.
- **EJ monogram** — now drawn as the negative-space badge; the argument moved to [its own section](#the-negative-space-ej) rather than staying here in one
  line. Still solves the favicon cleanly and still says nothing at all, which is either its virtue or its failure depending on how much work the mark is meant
  to do.

  stamp direction was chosen. It worked; it simply is not what this brand is. Recoverable from git — `docs/branding/mark-beanie.svg`, with its geometry

  `docs/branding/mark-overflow.svg`.
- **Pin + play** — a map pin whose hole is a play triangle. Legible, literal, and one of the most-used icon combinations in local-discovery products. Fails 4.
- **Ticket stub / wristband** — characterful and genuinely on-subject, but a torn edge is fine detail and fine detail is what 16 px removes. Fails 1 as drawn;
  would need to become a much blunter shape, at which point it is a rectangle.
- **The live dot** — a single pulsing circle. Unbeatable as a favicon and completely anonymous as a logo; it leans entirely on motion for meaning, and a
  favicon does not animate. **Kept as a UI motif**, where it is already earning its place (§5.5), and now folded into the beanie as the bobble.
- **Scan line** — a cropped barcode. §4b's own note: a horizontal-stripe silhouette is the same visual family as an equaliser, so it trades one crowded
  neighbourhood for the one next door. Fails 4.
- **Trainspotting poster language** — orange-and-black, mixed-weight condensed type, numbered strip, stark white ground. **Not a mark**; it is a whole visual
  identity, so it belongs to [#374](https://github.com/enorm-labs/event-junkie/issues/374) rather than here, and adopting it would replace the UV-violet accent
  §5.1 records as shipped. The characters and the cast's likenesses are owned; the poster grammar is not.
- **Anything waveform, heartbeat, soundbar or equaliser** — the neighbourhood the pulse mark has to leave. Not a judgement on any individual execution.

## Adding an idea

1. **Write it as one sentence describing a silhouette.** If that takes two sentences it will not survive 16 px.
2. **Score it against the five criteria** and add the row. Be honest about the `✗`s; an idea with a known fatal flaw is more useful recorded than argued away.
3. **Draw it** as a monochrome SVG in `docs/branding/`, `currentColor`, no gradient, with the geometry notes in a comment so the next person can redraw it
   rather than only nudge it. Every committed file does this — copy the shape of their comments.
4. **Search it for collisions**, and record the date and what you found even when the answer is nothing. Two searches, because they cover different risks:
   **TMview / eSearch plus image search** takes an uploaded PNG and ranks visually similar _registered_ marks across ~57 million figurative applications, free
   and without an account; a **reverse image search** (Google Lens, TinEye) is what surfaces an _unregistered_ mark in active use, which is exactly what
   SprintPulse was. §4a is the argument for why:
   _"we noticed and changed it before launch"_ is a materially better position than a recollection.
5. **Render it at 16, 32 and 180 px, light and dark, and look at it** — not at the SVG. Nothing automated in this repository will tell you the favicon turned
   to mud.
